## Prior-round audit

The release-driven redesign **preserves every architectural conclusion of round 36** and invalidates only its attestation mechanism. Nothing in the admission/turn/sequencer arithmetic, fence dispositions, publication and response ceilings, retained-response accounting, S3c grouped/cohort window, or reclaim ceiling depends on `read_uncommitted`; the grep confirms no remaining plan clause does, and protocol step 5 (`:677`) now forbids issuing it. Round 36's eight NOTEs and their dispositions stand.

Three prior conclusions are invalidated. First, the round-27→36 premise that effective reader isolation could be attested by pragma readback is dead: `PRAGMA read_uncommitted` returns no row on 0.7.0 and 0.7.2. Substituting semantic proof is the right correction and is strictly stronger evidence — `read_uncommitted` is a shared-cache-only SQLite knob that never was the isolation guarantee, so its removal creates no migration or compatibility gap (no persisted state, no schema, no other clause). Second, the round-7/8/22 lineage assumed the current serving reader was `query_only`-protected; it is not, and that is a v0.31.21 defect rather than evidence of dirty reads. Third, round 36's `APPROVE`/`YES` is superseded for S-0 only. Separately, the addendum's recorded `fresh_value=after` was already an over-claim before this round, since it reads the writer's own connection.

## Summary

The revised S-0 sequence is correctly ordered and non-circular in the direction it tests: the first SELECT provably follows the writer's uncommitted update (readers signal ready after `BEGIN` with no read, and the phase gate opens only after the `UPDATE` returns), the post-commit re-read genuinely distinguishes snapshot isolation from statement-level read-committed, and supported readback plus semantic proof is a sound replacement for the unavailable `read_uncommitted` row. It is not yet *sufficient* for S3r/S5, because it proves stability without proving freshness on any reader connection — the "fresh connection" check runs on the writer, and no pooled connection is ever shown to observe a commit in a snapshot opened after it, which is precisely the property the exact-coverage protocol and the 16+8 reused pooled connections rest on. Two further weaknesses reduce the proof's force: a single-row uncommitted change may never become shared state, making the no-dirty-read result potentially vacuous for an 800-item apply, and clause 6 ships a live serving-reader behavior change inside a proof slice that runs before any non-regression baseline, in a connection mode (autocommit, product busy timeout) the gate never exercises. Fix the freshness/reuse phase, make the uncommitted writer representative, and re-scope or gate the serving-reader correction; the remaining notes fit inside the named acceptance criteria.
